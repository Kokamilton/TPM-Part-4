# Case Study 3 — FICO → Jonas: Vendor Crisis Resolution Mid-Compliance Audit

**Company:** American Express  
**Role:** RMS System Owner / Product Owner, ACORN  
**Timeline:** 2021 — Discovery through full resolution  
**Stakes:** Two production systems at risk of discontinuation · Active compliance audit blocked · Dual contract void · Legal and regulatory exposure

---

## The Challenge

On May 5, 2021, FICO publicly announced the divestiture of its Collections & Recovery business to Jonas Software — a subsidiary of Constellation Software Inc. American Express did not receive advance notice.

The discovery was not made through a vendor notification. It was made through resistance — FICO began pushing back on requests for compliance documentation during the annual audit assessment process, citing that they no longer owned the data. The disclosure came only when forced through those conversations.

This created an immediate dual crisis.

**Crisis 1 — Compliance audit blocked.** The annual audit assessment — covering 16 policy areas including Information Security, Risk Management, Network Security, Encryption, Software Security, and Service Continuity — could not proceed with FICO as vendor of record. The actual data owner was now Jonas Software, an entity that had not been vetted, contracted, or approved as an AmEx vendor.

**Crisis 2 — Contracts void.** The DM9 Master Service Agreement and Statement of Work had expired in 2018. FICO had continued invoicing AmEx for annual maintenance — approximately $89,778.42 — without a formal contract or PO in place. The contract situation required immediate remediation alongside the broader vendor transition.

**Crisis 3 — Platform discontinuation threat.** Throughout the resolution process, Jonas was simultaneously threatening to discontinue support for both RMS (the legacy mainframe holding active collections data) and DM9 before ACORN was ready to absorb the full migration — meaning two production systems could go dark while a compliance audit was unresolved and a new vendor was being onboarded.

I owned full resolution end-to-end as the formal RMS system owner.

---

## My Role

As the formally designated RMS system owner — a sign-over that had placed my name on system ownership documents and granted me approval authority — I was the accountable owner for this resolution. No one directed me to take this on. The discovery came through my attempts to coordinate audit documentation. The resolution required my ownership because I was the only person with full context across the system, the vendor relationships, the compliance timeline, and the active ACORN program dependencies.

---

## What I Did

### Discovery and Immediate Escalation

The disclosure came through structured pressure during audit preparation — FICO's resistance to providing compliance documentation was the signal. I identified what had happened, confirmed the acquisition through independent research, and immediately escalated to leadership with a complete picture of the dual exposure: the audit could not continue and the contract situation required urgent remediation.

### Multi-Track Resolution — Executed Simultaneously

I managed five concurrent workstreams under active compliance pressure, holding all parties to a 1-day SLA on documentation exchanges throughout:

**Track 1 — Tri-party NDA Coordination**
Coordinated a tri-party NDA between American Express, Deloitte & Touche (the audit firm), and Jonas Software — enabling the annual audit to continue under the new vendor structure while the full onboarding process completed. This was the unblocking step; without it, the audit could not proceed regardless of how quickly Jonas was onboarded.

**Track 2 — Enterprise Vendor Onboarding**
Led the full Global Supply Management (GSM) vendor onboarding process for Jonas — covering:
- Legal review and Third-Party Lifecycle Management
- Risk Assessment Questionnaire (submitted and approved August 5, 2021)
- Due Diligence review
- Annual ORC Vendor Certification
- Process Risk Self-Assessment

**Track 3 — Dual Contract Execution**
Negotiated and executed separate contracts for two distinct systems:
- **RMS contract** transferred from FICO to Jonas — covering the legacy mainframe still holding active collections data
- **DM9 contract** — annual maintenance negotiated at $89,778.42 plus tax through March 16, 2022, with a 5% increase to $94,491.79 plus taxes for the following year
- 3-month contract extension executed October 28, 2021 to maintain continuity

**Track 4 — Compliance Audit Completion**
Maintained continuity of the annual audit assessment across all 16 policy areas despite the vendor disruption — ensuring documentation, access, and review could proceed once the tri-party NDA was in place.

**Track 5 — Platform Discontinuation Risk Management**
Managed ongoing Jonas threats to discontinue RMS and DM9 support while simultaneously running active ACORN product development — ensuring neither production system went dark before the migration was ready.

## What Made This Hard

Three things were true simultaneously throughout this resolution:

1. The vendor being onboarded was the same vendor threatening to discontinue the systems being audited
2. The audit could not proceed without the new vendor being approved, but the new vendor had not been vetted
3. Active ACORN product development was continuing in parallel across both platforms

The resolution required holding five concurrent workstreams, maintaining 1-day SLA across all parties, managing a vendor relationship under duress, and keeping the active ACORN program on track — all while the formal organizational cover for the decision-making was limited, because the situation had been discovered rather than assigned.

The decisions made here were principal-level calls under conditions most product managers never encounter: legal exposure, contract voids, compliance deadline pressure, vendor coercion, and active production risk — resolved without organizational direction and with subsequent leadership affirmation.

## What This Demonstrates for Hiring Managers

This case study is specifically relevant for roles that require:
- **Vendor risk management** with real legal and compliance stakes — not theoretical risk frameworks
- **Principal-level ownership** in ambiguous, high-pressure situations
- **Multi-track execution** across legal, compliance, procurement, and technical workstreams simultaneously
- **Judgment under pressure** — making the call, owning the outcome, and having it affirmed

For FAANG and Financial Services roles at the senior TPM level, the question is not whether you can build a roadmap — it is whether you can be trusted with the situations that don't fit the roadmap. This is one of those situations.
